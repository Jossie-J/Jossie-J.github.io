  <h1 id="about-title"><em>Hi!</em></h1>
  <p>Thanks for stopping by, I’m <strong>Jiaxun Jossie JIANG (蒋佳迅)</strong>.</p>
  <p>I am an MSc student in <strong>Geoinformatics Engineering at Politecnico di Milano</strong> and a visiting student in <a href="https://panhui.people.ust.hk/">Prof. Pan Hui</a>’s <a href="https://mc2.hkust-gz.edu.cn/people">MC² Lab</a> at <strong>The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou)</strong>. I work closely with <a href="https://pzhang.cn/">Pu Zhang</a> on social sensing and spatial analysis of environmental policy and urban service requests. My visit began in May 2026 and is scheduled to continue through December 2026.</p>
  <p>My research uses spatial data, digital traces, and administrative records to study how public concerns and urban conditions vary across places. I am interested in connecting computational social science with geospatial analysis to support urban governance and environmental decision-making.</p>
  <p>I have finalized my master’s thesis on the <strong>2025 Los Angeles wildfires</strong>, supervised by <a href="https://www.dica.polimi.it/en/il-dipartimento/persone/docenti/daniele.oxoli/">Prof. Daniele Oxoli</a> and co-supervised by <a href="https://panhui.people.ust.hk/">Prof. Pan Hui</a>, and plan to defend it in <strong>December 2026</strong>. I received my bachelor’s degree in Computer Science and Technology from Nanning Normal University.</p>

      <h3 id="la-wildfires-title">Spatial Damage and Permit Entry after the 2025 Los Angeles Wildfires</h3>
      <p class="homepage-project__meta">MSc thesis finalized · Defense planned for Dec 2026 · Journal extension in preparation</p>
      <p>My MSc thesis examines residential damage in the Eaton and Palisades fires and the timing of first recorded permit applications after the Eaton Fire. It combines spatial and administrative data to study where severe damage coincides with limited early application activity. The journal extension focuses on comparable application milestones across city and county permit systems, with plans to test whether models transfer between the two fires.</p>
      <p><strong>MSc research:</strong> Data integration, spatial and time-to-entry analysis, and thesis writing. Advised by Daniele Oxoli and co-advised by Pan Hui.</p>

      <span class="homepage-project__status homepage-project__status--review">Under review</span>
      <h3 id="nyc-311-title">Predictive stability but explanatory sensitivity across urban grid resolutions: Evidence from NYC 311 service requests</h3>
      <p class="homepage-project__meta"><cite>Applied Geography</cite> · Collaborative research with Pu Zhang</p>
      <p>This study examines how 2018 NYC 311 service requests vary with urban conditions across 300 m and 1,000 m grids. We use spatial validation, XGBoost with SHAP, and residual diagnostics to compare what the models predict and how those predictions are explained at each scale.</p>
      <p><strong>My role:</strong> I prepared data and contributed to writing the manuscript.</p>
      <details class="homepage-project__reference">
        <summary>Authors and methods</summary>
        <p>Corresponding author: Pu Zhang. Co-authors: Jiaxun Jiang and Pan Hui.</p>
        <p>We log-transform the number of requests in each grid cell and model it using urban predictors. Spatial GroupKFold and repeated spatial holdouts test prediction across areas. SHAP and out-of-fold residual diagnostics help compare the models at the two resolutions.</p>
      </details>
